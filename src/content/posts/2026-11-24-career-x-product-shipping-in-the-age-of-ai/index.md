Every product category eventually faces a platform shift. Cloud did it to on-premise infrastructure. Mobile did it to the desktop web. I lived through the first one - I started my career racking servers, and today I work almost entirely in the cloud. Born on premise, living in the cloud.

Careers are facing their own platform shift right now. AI has not replaced knowledge workers. It has changed what knowledge work is worth.

In [episode six](?post=career-x-product-sponsors-advocates-and-politics) we covered the people who carry your product. This episode is about the market itself: what happens to your value proposition when everyone has a PhD, a strategist, and a VP in their pocket.

## When answers become cheap

For decades, the scarce resource in a company was expertise. If you knew how to design the network, model the business case, or write the policy, you were valuable because few others could.

Today, a capable AI assistant can produce a credible first draft of almost any of those in minutes. That does not make expertise worthless. It makes **average expertise** cheap.

When the cost of producing an answer drops toward zero, the value moves up the stack:

| Commoditising | Becoming more valuable |
| --- | --- |
| Producing a first draft | Knowing which draft matters |
| Answering the question | Framing the right question |
| Knowing the facts | Judging what to do with them |
| Doing one step well | Owning the outcome end to end |
| Individual output | Trust, context, and relationships |

The products that thrive after a platform shift are the ones that reposition around what the new platform cannot do.

## The high-impact individual contributor

Elena Verna's essay [IC work is the new career flex](https://www.elenaverna.com/p/ic-work-is-the-new-career-flex) captures a shift I recognise from my own path. She describes the high-impact IC: someone who can take a project that delivers business value from start to finish, on their own, often after having led teams.

For a long time, the only way to scale your impact was to scale your headcount. Managing more people was the proxy for doing more. AI is breaking that proxy. One person with strong judgement, deep context, and good tools can now do work that used to require a small team.

I made that move deliberately. After building and leading a 36-person technical organisation across EMEA, I stepped back into an individual contributor role in the Office of the CTO. It was not a retreat. It was a bet on where leverage is moving. The leadership skills did not disappear. They became the multiplier on the craft.

## Your new value proposition

Go back to the Career Product Canvas from [episode one](?post=career-x-product-you-are-the-product). Look at the value proposition block and ask a sharper question:

> What can I do that the next-best alternative - a peer **with** AI - cannot?

Not "a peer". Not "an AI". A peer with AI. That is your real competition now.

Five capabilities I see differentiating people in this market:

**1. Problem framing.** Most failed AI transformations I have seen did not fail because the technology was not ready. They failed because they jumped to a solution before defining the problem. The person who frames the problem well sets the direction for everyone else - and every AI tool they use.

**2. Judgement and taste.** AI can generate ten options. Someone has to decide which one is right for this customer, this constraint, this moment. Taste is pattern recognition built from experience. It cannot be prompted into existence.

**3. Context.** Models know the public world. You know the organisation: its history, its politics, its unwritten constraints, who actually decides. Context is a moat.

**4. Trust.** Executives do not buy answers. They buy confidence from people they trust. Trust is built over years and lost in a meeting. AI does not have a reputation. You do.

**5. End-to-end ownership.** Generating a plan is easy. Getting it adopted, funded, shipped, and measured requires someone who will carry it through the organisation. That is still deeply human work.

## Using AI as your product team

Here is the reframe I find most useful: AI is not your competitor. It is your product team.

- **Your research analyst.** Synthesise sources, map a domain, find counterarguments.
- **Your editor.** Tighten the executive summary, sharpen the one-sentence impact statement.
- **Your sparring partner.** Stress-test your plan before your skip-level does.
- **Your coach.** Rehearse a difficult conversation. Prepare for a promotion discussion.

The people who will win are not those who avoid AI or those who outsource their thinking to it. They are the ones who use it to raise their own standard - more options considered, more rigour applied, more time freed for the human work only they can do.

## The inner game of a platform shift

Platform shifts create fear. Fear of irrelevance. Fear of being found out. Fear that the skills you spent fifteen years building no longer count.

Michael Gervais often returns to the idea that we cannot control the environment, only how we respond to it. Toto Wolff has spoken about how setbacks and rule changes in Formula 1 are not a reason to complain but a reason to out-learn everyone else. The regulations change for every team at the same time. The teams that adapt fastest win the next era.

The same is true here. The rules changed for everyone. Your advantage is how quickly you learn the new game.

## The unspoken

**"I am an expert in X" is a weakening position.** "I solve problem Y for customer Z, using whatever tools work best" is a strengthening one.

**AI exposes shallow work.** If your value was mostly in producing documents, decks, or code that nobody deeply needed, it will become visible quickly.

**AI also amplifies strong work.** People with real judgement and context get more leverage than ever. The gap between good and exceptional is widening, not closing.

**Headcount is losing its status.** The number of people you manage is becoming a weaker signal of impact. What you deliver, end to end, is becoming the stronger one.

## Low, good, exceptional

| | Low | Good | Exceptional |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AI stance | Ignores or fears it | Uses it to go faster | Uses it to raise the standard and reach new scope |
| Value | Produces output | Produces quality output | Frames problems and owns outcomes end to end |
| Differentiation | Knowledge | Skill | Judgement, context, and trust |
| Leverage | Personal | Team | Organisation, through assets and influence |

## Try this week

1. **Rewrite your value proposition** against a peer with AI. What remains distinctly yours?
2. **Pick one recurring task** and redesign it with AI as your product team. Use the time saved for problem framing or relationship work.
3. **Choose one end-to-end outcome** you could own entirely - from problem definition to measured result - and start.

> When answers are free, judgement is the product.
